# SPACE-TIME

**SPACE-TIME** is Helen Edgar's monotropism-informed framework for Autistic people. It names what a place has to hold — sensory attunement, predictability, acceptance, communication, empathy, and then togetherness, insiderness, meaning-making, embodiment — if an Autistic person is going to do more there than endure it.

Helen co-created Cavendish Space, and SPACE-TIME and this deck rest on the same premise: the thing that has to change is the environment. So they fit together the way a brief fits a build.

>> SPACE-TIME says what has to be true of a place. The cards are how a person tells you whether it is. The zone builder is where you put it.

The framework was first published as [SPACE-TIME: A Monotropism Informed Framework for Autistic People](https://autisticrealms.com/space-time-a-monotropism-informed-framework-for-autistic-people/) at Autistic Realms. It is carried here with Helen's agreement and dual-licensed — see Lineage and license at the foot of this page.

## What it joins together

Monotropism is the account of Autistic attention set out by Dinah Murray, Mike Lesser and Wenn Lawson (2005): attention flows deeply into a few things at a time. That makes for rich flow and deep meaning. It also makes an unfitting environment expensive, because an interruption is not a small cost when attention arrives in tunnels rather than spread thin (Heasman et al., 2024).

SPACE-TIME roots two existing frameworks in that account.

- **SPACE** — Sensory, Predictability, Acceptance, Communication, Empathy — from the Autistic SPACE framework (Doherty, McCowan and Shaw, 2023; McGoldrick et al., 2025). The foundations: what has to hold before anything else is possible.
- **TIME** — Togetherness, Insiderness, Meaning-Making, Embodiment — from the eight dimensions of care in an experience-sensitive approach (McGreevy et al., 2024, building on Todres, Galvin and Holloway, 2009). What belonging takes, once the foundations hold.

The name is doing more than spelling something. Space and time are the two things an Autistic person is most often short of and least often offered: somewhere to be, and long enough to be there. In Helen's words:

> Safe spaces, flexible time and supportive environments help meet Autistic people's needs across the lifespan.

## How to build a Cavendish Space with it

SPACE-TIME is a brief. It is not an audit of a person, and running it produces a list of things to change, never a list of people to watch. Four steps, and the deck does the middle two.

1. **Walk the room against the nine.** Take the table above and ask, for each element, what in this place already answers it. Most rooms answer Sensory and Predictability in part, Communication rarely, and Togetherness, Insiderness, Meaning-making and Embodiment almost never — not because nobody cares, but because nothing in the building was ever asked to.
2. **Ask the people who are in it.** Hand out [the deck](deck.html). This is the step that cannot be guessed at: attunement means attuning to a particular bodymind, and insiderness means the inside view is the account of record. Cards lie face-down; turning one up is the consent.
3. **Turn the spread into zones.** [Zone a room](rooms.html) takes the five places and the seventeen conditions and tells you what this room can hold and what it can't. What it can't is the honest part — a room that cannot hold a cave should say so rather than pretend.
4. **Build the smallest thing first.** [The implementation guidebook](implementation.html) turns a named need into a change to the space, on any budget. A blanket over a table is a cave. Permission to leave is a way out.

Then leave it open. A place built to a brief once is a place built for whoever was in the room that day; SPACE-TIME is a shape to keep checking against, not a certificate to hang up.

## Where the deck stops, and why

**Nobody is ever asked to name an element.** The deck cards states and needs in the plainest words we can find — *headphones*, *a way out*, *no rush*. It does not card the framework. Asking a person to point at "insiderness" would hand them the facilitator's vocabulary and call it their voice, which is the whole failure the deck is built to avoid. The mapping above runs one way: from what somebody laid down, to what the room owes them.

**SPACE-TIME is not a scale.** Nine elements with a letter each is an invitation to score a room out of nine, and from there to score a person out of nine, and from there to a chart on the wall with names on it. There is no count here, the same as there is no count in a spread or in a [group brief](group-needs.html). An element that a place does not answer is a thing to build, not a mark against anybody standing in it.

**It reads across every setting, not only clinical ones.** The two frameworks under it came from healthcare and education, and it would be easy to let that make this a professional's instrument. It isn't. The elements describe a kitchen, a workplace, a classroom, a conference, and a Sunday afternoon equally well, and a person can run it on their own home without anybody's permission.
