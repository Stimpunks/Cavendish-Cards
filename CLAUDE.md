## What this is

Cavendish Cards is a free, open (CC0), neuroaffirming pictorial prompt-card deck by Stimpunks Foundation. A person of any age SHOWS how they feel and what they need by pointing at or laying pictures — **no reading required, no scoring, no matching, no right answer, no winning.** The person controls sharing: cards lie face-down by default, and turning one up *is* the act of consent. One card = one Markdown file; the deck grows in the open, community-authored. It comes from the Cavendish Space model and the ARLES design method.

It is **not** a screening/assessment tool and **not** an AAC board (see Decision log). A spread is a *design brief for the environment*, not a behavior report.

## Values (non-negotiable)

- **Broken systems, not broken people.** Counter-deficit throughout. A card names a need, a state, or a change to the environment — never the person as the problem.
- **Neurodiversity paradigm**, not pathology. Identity-first language; always **capitalize Autistic and Disabled**.
- **Human/community-made art only — no AI art.** Guidebook/Notes are human-authored. **One carve-out, added 2026-09-16:** until human art arrives, every card carries a *placeholder vector illustration* drawn in code in `scripts/card-art.py`. Shapes with coordinates, hand-written and reviewable like any other source — never a generative-image tool. The card face itself stopped saying so on 2026-09-17, when the deck became something people print; the claim now lives in the repo and on the site instead. The call for art is still open and still the goal; see the Decision log.
- **Nothing about us without us.** Helen Edgar (Autistic Realms) owns the values-heavy and Autistic-community calls (see Collaborators for the current, looser review pattern).

## The realms (as of this snapshot — generators compute exact counts; check `cards/`)

- **Places** (~6): the cave, the campfire, the watering hole, the library, the habitat (+ your own). The five Cavendish zones. Fixed.
- **Weather** (~23): inner weather, the whole range good and hard. **Curated display order** (gentle range, no headings, no good/bad split).
- **What helps** (~34): the niche-construction realm; the largest on purpose. Grouped into ~12 sense **signposts** in its single-realm view (Being in charge, Sound, Light & looking, Touch, Pressure, Temperature, Movement, Mouth & nose, Space & enclosure, Telling & talking, People & time, Make your own).
- **Lily pads** (~10): transitions/moments. Renders in `web/app.js` as a normal browsable realm, same as any other family — the old always-on "moments strip" has been retired (`MOMENTS` in `build-site.py` is now an empty set).
- **Growers** (~4): dandelion/tulip/orchid resilience metaphor. Optional.
- **Kind words** (~10): affirmations. App label **"Kind words"** (subtitle "Also called Love Locutions or Love Languages"); the **guidebook keeps "Love Locutions."** Reframed as **self-affirmation / self-advocacy** — **given to someone, or claimed for yourself**, never earned, never a reward. Folder/slug stays `love-locution`.
- **Interaction** (~6): the Autistic community's red/yellow/green communication badges (ANI/Autreat/Sinclair 1996). The one exception to face-down default — meant to be shown; has its own distinct card back.
- **Blank** (1): draw your own. Every realm also carries a `your-own` card.

## Card schema & conventions

- `cards/<realm>/<slug>.md`: `# name` (lowercase, deck-facing), `## Family` (folder-matching key), `## Image cue`, `## Prompt` (`—` for Kind words), optional `## Reflection` (per-card override), `## Notes` (guidebook entry — metaphor / what it names / how to hold; "When this card is out…"; **describe the card, never the person**), optional `## Pattern` (facilitator-layer Stimpunks Pattern map; **never card-facing** — stripped from `cards.json`), `----`, `## License` (CC0).
- **Notes carry a reframe, not just a label** — this is the anti-AAC feature. Credit lineage in Notes where relevant (Adkin & Gray-Hammond for monotropism terms; Nick Walker/NQLS for embodiment; Erin/@EisforErin for tendril theory; ANI/Sinclair 1996 for Interaction; Cavendish Space for intermittent collaboration).
- Voice: short declarative sentences, stakes first, no hedging, no marketing register, em-dashes and fragments for weight. US spelling. Deck-facing copy lowercase.
- Generators in `scripts/` read `cards/**` by folder. `build-all.py` runs them all. Reflection pools, display labels, signpost groups, curated order, subtitles, and guidebook realm-notes are constants at the top of `build-site.py`; the starter deck's curated order + play modes in `build-starter-deck.py`; the markdown guidebook's realm-notes in `build-guidebook.py`.
- Reflection questions: hybrid (per-realm pool in `build-site.py` + optional per-card `## Reflection` override). A spread **distributes** questions across the pool so they don't repeat.
- **Hand-authored pages** (no rebuild, Netlify serves as-is): `web/index.html` is the **threshold** — three doors, deliberately thin on prose; `web/deck.html` is the card player (it was `index.html` until 2026-09-15); `web/rooms.html` is the room zoner; `web/badges.html` is the interaction-badge maker; `web/space.html` is the condensed account of the model. Their shared shell is `web/styles.css`; `app.js` drives the deck, `rooms.js` the zoner, and `badges.js` the badge maker. **`web/print.html` is generated, not hand-authored** — `print_sheets()` in `build-site.py` imposes the whole deck nine-up and the page embeds every sheet inline, so it needs no nav copy of its own; only `web/print.js` (the two print buttons) is hand-written.
- **Standalone web pages** generated by `build-site.py` into `web/` (all gitignored; Netlify rebuilds): `guidebook.html` + `implementation.html` from the cards; `why.html`, `not-aac.html`, `origin.html`, `arles.html`, `example-spreads.html`, `group-needs.html`, `livable-worlds.html`, `privacy.html`, `changelog.html` from their `.md` sources via a small no-dependency `md_to_html`; that renderer knows `##`/`###`, `- ` lists, `> ` blockquotes, **`>> ` pull quotes** (a line the page is built around, set large — it emits `<p class="pull">`, *not* a `<blockquote>`, because the words are the page's own, and `>> ` was chosen because a plain Markdown reader degrades it to a nested blockquote; **promote a line into one, never duplicate it**, so the sentence exists once in the source), images, rules, and inline links/bold/code/em; `facilitator.html` by **reusing `build-facilitator-pdf.py`'s `parse()`** so the on-site sheet and the print PDF render from one source; and `404.html`, which Netlify serves with a 404 status for any unmatched path. All of them go through `_standalone_page()`, so the nav and footer come from one place — add new prose pages there rather than by hand.
- **Agent-readable representations** (also generated, also gitignored): `web/llms.txt` (curated index, llms.txt v2) and a `.md` source endpoint per prose page, published by `_write_llms_txt()` and `_write_md_endpoints()`. The `MD_ENDPOINTS` registry in `build-site.py` maps a `SITE_NAV` key to its Markdown source; `/why.html` is mirrored at `/why.md`, and so on. A value of `None` means the Markdown is built in-process rather than copied from the repo root — `implementation` and `guidebook`; `_write_md_endpoints()` takes those bodies in its `generated` dict. **The guidebook was excluded until 2026-09-16** because its Markdown came only from `build-guidebook.py`; `build-site.py` now calls `build-guidebook.build_markdown()` itself, so `guidebook.html`, `guidebook.md` and the tracked root copy are all rendered from `cards/**` in one build and cannot disagree. Curated llms.txt prose lives in `_LLMS_SUMMARY`/`_LLMS_BODY`/`_LLMS_SECTIONS` — update it when the information architecture changes; a stale llms.txt is worse than none.
- **`security.txt` is generated, not hand-written** (`_write_security_txt()`), because RFC 9116's required `Expires` field invalidates the file once it lapses. Every deploy pushes it 330 days out — under the RFC's one-year ceiling, with the most headroom that leaves. **The only failure mode left is ~11 months with no deploy**; if the project goes quiet, add a scheduled Netlify build. `Contact` is stimpunks@stimpunks.org and deliberately *not* the GitHub advisory URL, since private vulnerability reporting is disabled on the repo and that link would dead-end a reporter — re-check that if it ever gets enabled. `Policy` points at `privacy.html`, whose “Reporting a security problem” section is the human-readable half. **It is written to `web/security.txt`, not `web/.well-known/`:** Netlify does not publish dot-directories from the publish dir (verified — the well-known path 404'd on a real deploy while every sibling file served). `web/_redirects` rewrites `/.well-known/security.txt` to it with a **200, not a 301** — RFC 9116 wants the well-known URL itself to answer.
- **Paired constants kept in sync by hand:** `SCREENING` and `FRAMEWORKS`/`FRAMEWORKS_LEAD` appear in **both** `build-site.py` and `build-guidebook.py` and must match word-for-word (sync comment present). `SITE_NAV` in `build-site.py` is mirrored in **five** hand-authored breakbars (generated pages build theirs from `SITE_NAV` itself) — `web/index.html` (the threshold), `web/deck.html` (the deck), `web/rooms.html` (the zoner), `web/badges.html` (the badge maker), and `web/space.html` (the model) — keep all six copies in sync when adding a page. There is a parity check worth running after any nav edit: compare the hrefs in `SITE_NAV` against the `<details class="disclose sitenav">` menu in each of the five files; they must match in order and in count (18 items as of 2026-09-17). A new page also needs adding to `_SITE_PAGES` (sitemap), `_LLMS_SECTIONS` (llms.txt), and both service-worker lists in `_write_service_worker()` — the hash inputs and the precache — or a returning visitor never sees it offline. The `<link rel="describedby" … href="/llms.txt">` tag is likewise in all five hand-authored heads and in the generator's three head templates — eight copies; the matching `Link:` header is in `netlify.toml`. The Pattern-crosswalk slug registry lives in `build-guidebook.py`; `build-site.py` imports it. **The 17 `COND` ids in `rooms.js` are now a published contract:** `cavendish-cards-group-needs.md` lists every one of them for an AI to map into, and `rooms.html#asked=<ids>` is the link a group brief arrives on — rename or drop one and the doc, the link, and any brief already written go stale together. `_check_cond_parity()` in `build-site.py` compares the two on every build and prints a warning per mismatch to stderr — non-fatal, like the missing-face warning, so watch the build output. **Prose that cites cards is checked too:** `_check_card_citations()` reads the documents in `_CARD_CITING_DOCS` and warns when an italic list beside real cards holds something that is not a card, when a near-miss differs only by its article (`cave` for `the cave`), or when a list introduced by a realm word holds a card from another realm — which is how `tell me first`, a What helps card, got printed as a lily pad in both the facilitator sheet and the starter deck. It cannot catch an invented phrase in plain prose with no italics and no realm word near it; that still needs a read. The guidebook is deliberately excluded, since its card names come from `cards/**` beside the card they describe. `_check_group_example()` does the same job for the group-brief page's worked example, which twice contradicted the rules printed above it.

### Build gotchas (still live)

- **A group brief must never pre-tick the zoner.** A tick in `rooms.html` means *the room has this*; a brief means *somebody asked for this*. `rooms.js` renders `#asked=` as a separate overlay for that reason — pre-ticking would make the page announce zones the room cannot hold, which is the one thing it refuses to do. The asked list is read from the fragment and deliberately **not** written to `localStorage` (it is somebody's access needs), and it is re-sorted into `COND` order so a ranked link cannot smuggle a ranking through.
- **`clip-path` and `transform` must not share an element in the card face.** A `clip-path` resolves in the element's own user space, so a `<g clip-path transform>` clips against the *moved* coordinates and shears the top off every illustration (this shipped briefly and was only visible on cards whose art reached the window's top edge). `build-placeholders.py` keeps the clip on an outer untransformed `<g>` with the transform on an inner one.
- **A new card needs a motif in `scripts/card-art.py`,** registered in `MOTIFS` under `(family, slug)`. `build-placeholders.py` prints missing ones to stderr rather than failing, so watch the build output. Two same-size heads side by side above any horizontal line read as a frowny face — vary the sizes, heights, or drop the line.
- **No page here may use a `style` attribute.** The CSP is `style-src 'self'` with no `'unsafe-inline'`, which blocks inline style *attributes* as well as inline `<style>` elements — so a color set with `style="--sw:#1E8E3E"` silently does nothing in production while working fine on a local `http.server` that sends no CSP. Signal colors are classes in `styles.css` (`.sig-<slug>`), badge sheets are drawn with SVG presentation attributes, and `badges.js` sets the print `@page` box through `CSSStyleSheet.insertRule()`, which CSP does not govern. CSSOM writes (`el.style.foo = …`) are allowed; the attribute is not.
- **`img-src` allows `data:` for one reason only:** `badges.html` embeds a logo the user picks from their own disk. Anything else the site shows must be same-origin. An export path that inlines a `<style>` (the fonts written into a downloaded SVG) is built as a *string* and never inserted into the document — build it with DOM APIs and the CSP refuses it.
- **A print stylesheet that matches nothing fails silently.** `print.html` nests as `main.wrap > div.prose > section`, the hand-authored pages as `main > div.wrap > section`; a rule written for one shape hid nothing on the other, and the only symptom was a wasted ream. `styles.css` lists both. **Verify a print path rather than reading it:** render the page with the PDF venv's weasyprint, with the `printing-*` body class and an `@page` rule applied by hand, and count the pages — 15 for the deck's faces, 30 with backs, one per badge sheet. That is how the deck's sheets were caught paginating to 18 and the badge sheets were caught printing at 8.08in instead of 8.5.
- **Nothing that wraps a print sheet may have padding.** Both print paths put an SVG of an exact physical size on a page box of the same size, so `main`'s 28px and `.wrap`'s 20px are real millimetres: they pushed a 10.5in sheet onto a second page and shrank a badge below 3 × 4in. The shared `@media print` block strips them and renders each sheet at `width: auto; height: auto` — its own intrinsic size — rather than at 100% of its container.
- **Do not hoist the `weasyprint` import to module level in `build-facilitator-pdf.py`,** and do not change `parse()`'s `(title, kicker, blocks)` return shape — either one breaks the Netlify site build and weasyprint-less machines, because `build-site.py` reuses that `parse()` to render `facilitator.html`.
- **macOS PDF build** needs native libs (`brew install cairo pango gdk-pixbuf libffi`) plus a Homebrew-Python venv — Apple's system Python can never work, because macOS strips `DYLD_*` when launching a SIP-protected binary, so cffi never finds libcairo or libgobject. **`build-all.py` now handles this for you:** it looks for `~/.venvs/cavendish-pdf/bin/python` (override with `CAVENDISH_PDF_PYTHON`) and runs the PDF step with it, setting `DYLD_FALLBACK_LIBRARY_PATH` to Homebrew's lib dir in the child env — which survives, because dyld only strips it when launching a *protected* binary and the venv interpreter isn't one. So `python3 scripts/build-all.py` builds everything, the PDF included, from any Python. Create the venv once:
  ```bash
  brew install cairo pango gdk-pixbuf libffi
  /opt/homebrew/bin/python3 -m venv ~/.venvs/cavendish-pdf
  ~/.venvs/cavendish-pdf/bin/pip install weasyprint
  ```

## Decision log (settled — don't relitigate without reason)

- **A group brief never counts (2026-09-16).** Many spreads from one event can be turned into a single brief for the room. `cavendish-cards-group-needs.md` → `/group-needs.html` is the method, written so a coordinator can hand the page itself to whatever genAI they use as a skill description. Three rules, non-negotiable: **no score**; **the room is the subject** of every output sentence, never a person or a group; and **no counting** — not how many spreads named a thing, not sorted by frequency, never marked common, rare, or an outlier. Access is not a vote (one person needing a way out is as actionable as eight, because you build it once), and in a small group a count re-identifies. The AI **translates, never clusters**: into the 17 `COND` ids in `rooms.js` plus three non-room buckets (kit, permission, pacing), with an honest unmapped remainder. Authored branches keep the reframes that invented categories lose. **Never aggregated:** Weather (state, not need — and inferring a room change from a mood is profiling), Kind words, Interaction, **Growers** (self-description; a pile of it summarized is the closest this deck comes to a profile, and "give me my conditions and I bloom" is tempting to mine precisely because it sounds like it names conditions), and the lily pads that are passing states (`ready now`, `all done`). **Lily pads split three ways:** `coming back` maps (it literally asks the room to hold a place), the four pace requests map to `no-rush`, the two passing states are never aggregated, and `stuck`/`watch first` go to the remainder — named in the mapping table so none of it is inferred. **The brief must report what it had to drop** (kind only, never content, never how many): a silent drop teaches a coordinator the strip step worked when it did not. **Two remainders, never merged:** "the deck had no card for this" is a gap in the deck; "the method had no box for this" is a gap in the instructions. Places read as a direct request for a zone. Consent gets its own step ahead of the method: merge, strip names, drop the note field, say so before people lay cards, and know the AI is a third party. **No form POST, no endpoint, no storage** — `cavendish-cards-privacy.md`'s "no server-side code, no database" still holds and `netlify.toml`'s `form-action 'none'` is untouched. Logged for Helen's batch review.
- **Placeholder vector art is allowed; AI-generated art is not (2026-09-16).** The human-art rule stands for the deck's real art. But since the call for art opened, **zero** human pieces have been submitted, and the deck shipped 88 cards whose picture window was an empty dashed box — which defeats the entire premise, since a person is meant to *point at a picture*. So the same carve-out already in force for the favicon, the app icons, and the card frames now covers card faces: plain vector illustration, hand-written as code in `scripts/card-art.py`, replaced automatically the moment a finished face lands in `assets/cards/`. **No generative-image tool, ever** — these are shapes with coordinates, diffable and editable. Logged for Helen's batch review.
- **The `PLACEHOLDER ART · HUMAN ART WANTED` line came off the card face (2026-09-17).** It was the condition the carve-out above was granted on, so removing it is a real change and not a tidy-up. What changed is that the deck became something people *print*: `/print.html` hands anyone the whole deck at playing-card size, and a line of grey capitals across ninety-four cards is a line nobody can take off once they are cut. The card is for the person holding it, and that sentence was never addressed to them — it was addressed to us, and to anyone who might mistake a stand-in for finished art. **It still is said, just not on the card**: the call for art, the README, the playtest and template READMEs, and the site all carry it, which is where somebody who could actually send art will read it. Dropped everywhere rather than only on the print sheets, because a card that says one thing on paper and another on screen is two decks. `ART_DROP` is now computed from the art and window heights instead of hard-coded, since the offset existed to leave room for that line. Logged for Helen's batch review.
- **AI crawlers are welcomed explicitly, not blocked (2026-09-17).** `robots.txt` names ten vendor agents — GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ChatGPT-User, ClaudeBot, anthropic-ai, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, PerplexityBot, Bytespider, CCBot — each with `Allow: /`, generated from `AI_CRAWLERS` in `build-site.py`. **Why allow rather than block:** the deck is CC0, and you cannot dedicate work to the public domain and then object to it being read; `llms.txt` already *steers* models rather than shutting them out, so a `Disallow` would contradict a file the same generator writes. And the substantive reason, which is the one that decided it: if a model is going to say something about Autistic and Disabled people, better it read this deck than not — counter-deficit framing absent from training data means pathology language fills the gap. **This does not loosen the no-AI-art rule**, which governs what the deck *contains*, not who may read a public document; nor the no-third-parties rule, which is about what a visitor's browser loads. The preamble in `robots.txt` states the stance in the deck's voice, and says plainly that it is neither a security boundary nor a deletion request. Naming each agent rather than relying on `User-agent: *` is the point — a blanket rule cannot show that openness was chosen. Vendor names change; re-check the vendor docs rather than trusting the list's age. Logged for Helen's batch review.
- **The badge maker is the only place a realm gets its own tool, and Interaction is the only realm that could have one (2026-09-17).** Every other realm lies face-down until its person turns it up, so a page that prints them would print somebody's spread. Interaction is worn, shown, and flipped, so `web/badges.html` makes it at the four standard conference badge sizes — 4 × 3 in, A7, A6, 4 × 6 in, portrait or landscape — with the card's own name and line on each. - **No DNS CAA records** — researched 2026-09-17, not applied, because it is a DNS change in the Netlify UI rather than anything in this repo. Without CAA, any of ~50 public CAs may issue for cavendish.space. Records to add at the **apex of both `cavendish.space` and `cavendish.app`** (Netlify DNS supports the CAA type; TTL 300):
  ```
  0 issue     "letsencrypt.org;accounturi=https://acme-v02.api.letsencrypt.org/acme/acct/54403714"
  0 issuewild "letsencrypt.org;accounturi=https://acme-v02.api.letsencrypt.org/acme/acct/54403714"
  0 iodef     "mailto:stimpunks@stimpunks.org"
  ```
  That `accounturi` is Netlify's own published ACME account (RFC 8657), from their HTTPS docs — it pins issuance to Netlify, not just to Let's Encrypt. **Three traps, in order of how badly they bite:**
  1. **`issuewild` must permit Let's Encrypt, not `";"`.** The generic advice (including specification.website's starter set) says forbid wildcards — but Netlify DNS auto-provisions a **wildcard** cert, so `issuewild ";"` would break renewal. Verified: the live cert's SANs are `*.cavendish.app, *.cavendish.space, cavendish.app, cavendish.space`.
  2. **Both domains are on one certificate**, so a CA checks CAA for every name on it. Setting records on only `.space` leaves `.app` open to any CA; a mistake on *either* domain breaks HTTPS for *both*.
  3. **Failure is silent and delayed.** The current cert runs to 2026-12-14, so a bad record would not surface until renewal in mid-November. Verify with `dig CAA cavendish.space +short` and <https://sslmate.com/caa/> straight after adding, rather than waiting.

  If renewal ever does fail, relax to plain `0 issue "letsencrypt.org"` (drop `accounturi`) first — that still narrows ~50 CAs to one, and the `accounturi` pin is the part tied to a Netlify-internal value that could change.
