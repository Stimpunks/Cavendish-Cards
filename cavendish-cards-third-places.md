# A third place needs a cave

Not home. Not work or school. Somewhere you can be with other people without having to buy anything. That is a [third place](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/third-places/), and the public library has always been both a third place and a Cavendish Space.

Ryan Boren worked through where the two ideas overlap and where they part, on stimpunks.org: [A Third Place Needs a Cave: Cavendish Space as Third Place](https://stimpunks.org/2026/11/01/a-third-place-needs-a-cave/). This page carries the argument and the checklist that comes out of it.

>> The classic third place is a watering hole. A Cavendish Space is a third place with a cave and a campfire in it, and a door you are allowed to stand outside.

## What a third place is

The idea is Ray Oldenburg's, from *The Great Good Place* (1989). Home is the first place. Work or school is the second. The third is everywhere else people gather by choice: the café, the barbershop, the pub, the library, the corner store with chairs out front.

The Stimpunks glossary lists what makes one:

- neutral ground
- a leveling place
- conversation space
- open and accessible
- regulars
- low profile
- a playful mood
- a home away from home

Third places are disappearing, and Oldenburg saw it coming. A *radical* third place, in the glossary's sense, is open in hours and location **and** makes basic accommodations for the whole range of people who might come in.

## What a Cavendish Space adds

Cavendish Space is named for Henry Cavendish, who needed his cave — his house and his laboratory — and also, now and then, a few peers at the Royal Society's Monday Club. That was his campfire. A friend, Charles Blagden, carried his ideas out to the watering holes of science so that he did not have to go himself.

Both models are about places that hold people without owning them, and belonging without a transaction. They were drawn from different bodies, and that is where they part. [What is a Cavendish Space?](space.html) has the five places and the rule underneath them: a zone is a place, not a state. You zone the room. The room does not zone anybody.

## The eight characteristics, read through Cavendish

Most of Oldenburg's list holds. One item breaks, and how it breaks is the whole argument.

### Neutral ground

Oldenburg says nobody there is burdened by the role of host or guest, and people come and go when they please. That is the nearest he gets to Cavendish Space. A Cavendish reading takes it one step further: neutral ground makes no claim on how you are. Nobody reads how regulated you look and sends you somewhere. You go where you need to. The room holds still.

### Leveling place

A third place levels rank — the banker and the bricklayer at the same bar. But a loud bar does not level the senses. A room can erase every status marker in it and still sort people by how much noise, light and crowd their bodies can take. **Leveling has to include sensory safety, or it is leveling for the people the room already fits.**

### Conversation space

Here it breaks. In Oldenburg's account the talk is the point:

> The sustaining activity is conversation…
>
> — Ray Oldenburg, *The Great Good Place*

Take the conversation away and, by that account, the place stops being one. For a great many Autistic people, Cavendish among them, conversation is a price of entry we cannot always pay. His peers at the Monday Club worked out how to reach him:

> The way to talk to Cavendish is never to look at him, but to talk as it were into a vacancy, and then it is not unlikely but you may set him going.
>
> — Francis Wollaston, as quoted in Steve Silberman's *NeuroTribes*

He went. He stood at the edge and listened. He talked when the talk came sideways. He was a regular at a third place on terms it had not been built for.

The Autistic community has names for those terms. [Parallel play](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/parallel-play/) is being alone together. [Body doubling](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/body-doubling/) is somebody there while you work, not helping and often not speaking. [Parallel Presence](https://stimpunks.org/patterns/library/parallel-presence/), Pattern 29 in the Stimpunks Pattern Library, puts it plainly: presence is participation, and a place that only counts talking has measured one style of connection, not connection.

So a Cavendish third place is a presence space first. Conversation is welcome there and never owed. Being near people is the whole point, and joining in stays optional.

### Open and accessible

Opening hours and a handy location are where the classic list stops. Cavendish Space starts at the door, because the biggest barriers are usually not inside a space but at the edges between spaces. The dread that lands at a doorway has a name, [threshold anxiety](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/threshold-anxiety/), and institutions build the thresholds that cause it.

A third place is open when you can find out what is behind the door before you open it. When you can watch from the edge first. When you can leave without anybody noticing, and come back next week without explaining. The small places that make that possible are what the deck calls lily pads.

### Regulars

Regulars are how a place remembers itself, and how it quietly closes. The Monday Club worked for Cavendish because its regulars changed how they talked so he could come in. That is the test: not how warm the regulars are to each other, but whether they adapt to the person at the edge. Regulars who keep a culture of [psychological safety](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/psychological-safety/) are the radical part. Regulars who keep a culture of in-jokes and volume are a clique with a lease.

### Low profile

A third place is plain and unpretentious, and that turns out to be a sensory virtue. Low profile is low stimulation. In Cavendish terms it is the habitat: the steady, predictable ground that makes every other place reachable.

### Playful mood

Yes — and play often looks like an [infodump](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/infodump/), a stim, or two people building separate things side by side. A playful mood that only counts banter misses most of how we play.

### Home away from home

At its best a third place offers the comfort and support of a good home. Cavendish had that at his house in Clapham. Almost nobody else does, which is the point of the next section.

## Where the coffeehouse fails

The bustling coffeehouse is the picture most people hold of a third place, and it is what open-plan offices copied. Annie Murphy Paul names what that misses:

> The value of such interactions can be extracted only if it is also possible, when necessary, to avoid interacting at all.
>
> — Annie Murphy Paul, *The Extended Mind*

It is the same finding as [intermittent collaboration](https://stimpunks.org/glossary/intermittent-collaboration/): people solve hard problems best when time together is broken up by time apart. The watering hole works because there is a cave to go back to. **A third place with no cave is a watering hole with no shade.** People can visit. Not everyone can stay.

The library already knows this. Quiet corners, tables for gathering, open floors to wander, nobody checking whether you are being social correctly, nobody asking you to buy anything to justify being there. A cave, a campfire and a watering hole under one roof — which is why so many of us have a library origin story, and so few of us have a pub one.

## The estate and the commons

Cavendish could build the conditions he needed because he was rich. His house was his niche. The name Cavendish Space carries the argument that **the conditions that let a mind work should not require nobility.**

The third place is the public version of the estate: the room you get without owning one. That is why *without being required to buy stuff* is the line in the definition that matters most, and why losing third places hits Disabled and Neurodivergent people first and hardest. When the free room goes, the only Cavendish Space left is the one you can pay for.

A third place is commons infrastructure. So is a Cavendish Space. The Stimpunks pattern for [commons infrastructure](https://stimpunks.org/patterns/library/commons-infrastructure/) says what happens to that kind of thing: it is the first thing defunded and the last thing credited. Neither survives being treated as a nice extra.

## Building them anyway

Stimpunks builds them where it can. [Stimpunks.World](https://stimpunks.world/), a street of rooms online, builds every third place to the radical list:

- **[A Quiet Pint](https://stimpunks.world/a-quiet-pint.html)** — every pint alcohol-free first, in the same glass, so nobody can tell what anybody has. Nobody has to order to sit down.
- **[The Truck Stop](https://stimpunks.world/truckin-food-court.html)** — no prices. The trucks hand things out, and nobody asks who you are or why you are hungry.
- **[The Community Library](https://stimpunks.world/community-library.html)** — you are a patron, not a customer.
- **[Cavendish Coworking](https://stimpunks.world/cavendish-coworking.html)** — a cave, a campfire and a watering hole as drop-in rooms. Every door says what its room is for before you knock. Nothing is locked. Camera and microphone can stay off.

Cavendish Coworking has the shortest summary of this whole page:

> A place can be open without being open-plan.
>
> — Cavendish Coworking, on Stimpunks.World

## Ask it of any third place

If you run a library, a game shop, a youth club, a church hall, a classroom or a Discord server, ask:

- **Is there a cave?** Somewhere to be alone in company, with no explanation owed.
- **Is there a campfire?** A small group, not a stage.
- **Is joining in optional?** Does being there count as taking part?
- **Can people read the door before they open it?** Can they watch from the edge first, and leave without a scene?
- **Does it cost anything to be there?** In money, in a diagnosis, in a login, in performing being social correctly?
- **Do the regulars change how they talk so somebody new can come in?**

These are questions about the place. They are never a score, and never a check on the people in it. [Zone a room](rooms.html) does the same job for one room, against all five places.

## Where the deck comes in

A spread is a design brief for the room, and a third place is a room. The What helps cards *let me come and go*, *a way out*, *parallel existence* and *less talking* ask for exactly what the classic list leaves out. The lily pads *watch first* and *coming back* are the door you can stand outside, and the place that is still yours next week. And the kind word *you don't have to talk* is the line a Cavendish third place would paint over its door.
